Business Funding Profile Development + Funding Preparation

You do not need to decide whether you need a grant, loan, investment, research program, state or local program, or another funding instrument before you begin.

  • Persistent Business Funding Profile
  • Funding-readiness assessment
  • Capital requirement and use-of-funds development
  • Multiple funding paths when appropriate
How the Process Works

Funding preparation begins with a Funding Development Interview.

The Funding Preparation Representative first develops a detailed understanding of the business. FDC then organizes that information into a persistent Business Funding Profile, identifies preparation gaps, develops the capital requirement, evaluates appropriate funding paths, and presents the business with options to consider.

Funding Paths

One business may require more than one source of capital.

FDC is designed to evaluate multiple potential paths instead of assuming a single funding source will satisfy the complete capital requirement.

GrantsPublic, private, targeted, and specialized grant programs.
Loans / DebtBusiness lending and debt-financing options appropriate to the objective.
Federal Non-DilutiveResearch, innovation, development, and other non-dilutive opportunities.
State & LocalState, local, regional, and development programs.
EquityInvestment paths where equity capital may be appropriate.
Equipment FinancingAsset- and equipment-specific financing strategies.
Tax Credits & IncentivesApplicable incentive and credit programs.
Blended StrategyMultiple sources allocated across the business capital requirement.
Business Profile Only

FDC stores the business funding profile—not personal financial documents.

The service is designed to organize structured business information used for funding preparation while maintaining a clear data boundary around personal financial records.

FDC Business Profile May Include

  • Business identity, history, products, services, and operating profile
  • Founder and management qualifications relevant to funding
  • Business assets, technology, and applicable IP information
  • Business costs, commercialization, financial metrics, and unit economics
  • Capital requirement, use-of-funds plan, readiness status, and funding paths

FDC Does Not Store

  • Personal bank statements
  • Personal tax returns
  • Personal credit reports
  • Personal investment statements
  • Other personal financial account documents
