The FDC Method

Funding preparation starts with understanding the business.

FDC does not begin by forcing the business to choose a funding product. The Funding Development Interview builds the profile first. Funding-path options come after the business, objective, evidence, costs, assets, commercialization position, and capital requirement are understood.

01

Funding Development Interview

A structured conversation captures the business history, funding objective, operating needs, founder background, investments, assets, commercialization status, and available business evidence.

02

Business Funding Profile

The interview develops a reusable business funding profile that grows over time and reuses information already available through BODA whenever appropriate.

03

Funding Preparation

FDC organizes the business facts, identifies readiness gaps, develops the capital requirement and use-of-funds plan, and prepares the business for funding-path evaluation.

Funding Paths

The right answer may be one source—or several.

A business capital requirement does not have to be satisfied by one funding source. FDC can develop multiple funding paths and blended strategies where the business facts support them.

GrantsPublic, private, targeted, and specialized grant opportunities.
Loans / DebtBusiness lending and debt-financing options appropriate to the objective.
Federal Non-DilutiveResearch, innovation, development, and other non-dilutive programs.
State & LocalPrograms administered by state, local, regional, and development organizations.
Investment CapitalEquity or other investment paths where appropriate to the business strategy.
Equipment FinancingCapital solutions tied to machinery, technology, equipment, and productive assets.
Tax Credits / IncentivesApplicable incentive programs identified as part of the broader capital strategy.
Blended StrategyMultiple complementary sources structured around the total capital requirement.

Information Boundary

Business funding development—not personal financial document storage.

FDC stores the structured business funding profile and preparation records needed to develop funding strategy. It is not designed as a repository for personal financial documents.

FDC Business Profile May Include

  • Business identity, history, products, services, and operations
  • Founder qualifications relevant to business funding
  • Business assets and applicable intellectual-property information
  • Business expenses, operating costs, commercialization information, and business financial metrics
  • Capital requirement, use-of-funds plan, readiness status, funding paths, and opportunity records

FDC Does Not Store

  • Personal bank statements
  • Personal tax returns
  • Personal credit reports
  • Personal investment statements
  • Copies of personal financial-account documents
